# Mettle's MVP proves deadline-driven inspection recovery

Mettle serves a small residential general contractor who has received a failed-inspection or correction notice and must coordinate several trades before a reinspection date.

## The problem

Today the contractor reads the notice, translates each citation into work for a trade, chases replies and photos through text messages, remembers the deadline, and manually assembles evidence. The failure is not a missing task list. It is the repeated coordination required while the contractor is already moving between jobs.

## The core bet

A contractor will delegate the recovery campaign if the system anchors every action to the notice, communicates through ordinary text messages, and reserves code interpretation and final approval for the contractor.

## The single MVP loop

1. The contractor uploads one failed-inspection notice.
2. Mettle extracts citations and quotes the notice's language.
3. Mettle proposes evidence requirements and assignments.
4. The contractor resolves any ambiguous citation.
5. Mettle follows up with trades against the reinspection clock.
6. Evidence is linked to citations and checked for sufficiency.
7. The contractor approves a reinspection-ready correction packet.

## What must be excellent

- **Notice anchoring:** every derived task and evidence request points back to the source citation.
- **Campaign behavior:** urgency and escalation change as the deadline approaches.
- **Principled deference:** ambiguous code language and final submission always stop for human judgment.
- **Demo clarity:** judges can see the agent handle many coordination events while the contractor makes only a few decisions.

## Explicitly out of scope

- permit discovery or submission;
- code-compliance certification;
- inspector impersonation or autonomous inspector contact;
- multi-company billing, estimating, scheduling, or CRM;
- native mobile applications;
- a general-purpose punch-list system;
- production SMS sending before an explicit live-demo safety review; and
- more than one representative notice format in the first demo.

## Demo success

The three-minute demo succeeds when one notice produces a visible recovery timeline, one insufficient response triggers a precise re-request, one ambiguous citation pauses for contractor judgment, urgency changes near the deadline, and the final packet is blocked until contractor approval.

## Evidence that would change the plan

The product wedge weakens if contractor interviews show either that multi-trade correction campaigns rarely last beyond the inspection day or that subcontractors will not reply to an unfamiliar SMS number. Until those interviews happen, the synthetic demo must remain useful and honest without claiming field validation.
